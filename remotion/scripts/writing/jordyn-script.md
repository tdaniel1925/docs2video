# JORDYN — the script

## The central claim

**You don't want an AI that acts for you. You want one that does the work
and still asks.**

Everything in the film sets that up, supports it, or lands it. A line that
does none of those three comes out, however good it sounds.

## Why this claim and not the old one

The first cut argued *"the perfect employee is a fantasy — we built her."*
That is a hiring argument, and it is not what the product is selling. It
also walks straight into the objection instead of answering it: an
autonomous employee who acts on your behalf is the thing buyers are
frightened of, and that cut spent forty seconds making her sound MORE
autonomous.

The site already knows better. Two promises repeat all over it:

  "Nothing sends without your OK. Ever."
  "Arrives already speaking your industry."

Those are both answers to the same fear — that handing work to an AI means
losing control of your own voice and your own name. So the film should
argue the thing the buyer is actually stuck on, and the product's real
shape is the resolution: **she does everything except the last inch.**

That also gives the film a reason to END somewhere, which the list of
features never did.

## The script, as prose

> It is not that you have too much to do. It is that all of it has to go
> through you.
>
> Sixty emails, and four of them matter. The invoice you did not send. The
> client who went quiet and you have not noticed yet. It all works — as
> long as you never stop.
>
> So you have thought about help. And then you have thought about what
> help actually costs. Not the salary. The six weeks of explaining. The
> watching over their shoulder. The things sent in your name before they
> knew what your name meant.
>
> That is the real reason you are still doing it yourself.
>
> Jordyn starts already knowing your business. Your industry's words on
> the first day. Your voice inside a week. She works the whole inbox
> overnight, so the four that matter are waiting on top. She writes the
> chase you have been putting off. She answers the phone in your name and
> books the appointment while they are still talking.
>
> And then she stops.
>
> Every email she writes is a draft until you press send. Every single
> one. Not a setting you can switch off. She does the work. You keep the
> last word.
>
> That is the part nobody else will give you.

## How it holds together

Read it aloud as one paragraph and it argues rather than lists. Each
movement hands to the next:

  1-2   the problem, stated precisely — the bottleneck is YOU, not volume
  3-4   why you have not fixed it — the cost of help is trust, not money
        ("that is the real reason" names the obstacle out loud)
  5     what she does — and it EARNS its place because 3-4 just set up
        "already knowing your business" as the answer to "six weeks of
        explaining". Without that setup it is a feature list.
  6     the turn. Three words, alone. The whole film pivots here.
  7     the claim, landed — the objection from 3-4 answered directly
  8     why it matters

No two lines can swap without breaking it. "And then she stops" is
meaningless before the work is described and redundant after the draft
line. "That is the real reason you are still doing it yourself" only lands
after the cost is named. The old script failed that test on every line.

## What the pictures have to do

The writing came first and the shots serve it, which is the opposite of
how the last one was built — there, each line was written to caption a
picture, which is exactly why every line was a fragment.

Two shots carry the whole thing and should be the ones that MOVE:

  - the turn ("And then she stops") — motion arriving at stillness
  - the last shot — the held beat the claim lands on

The rest can be stills moved by code. See jordyn-film.mjs.
